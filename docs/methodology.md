# Methodology

Monstats describes registered teams and observed results. It does not measure individual Pokémon combat performance or establish causal counter strength.

## Tournament population

Eligible events use a supported game and regulation, have at least 20 evidenced entrants, and fall within the reviewed regulation interval. Large events require at least 100 entrants. Official coverage uses Masters attendance. Selective top-team samples are excluded from whole-event usage.

Provider filters select compatible record suppliers. All pools deduplicated counts rather than averaging percentages. Verified original identities join compatible event copies; conflicting or ambiguous joins are excluded. All sheets includes unknown sheet status; open/closed filters require organizer evidence.

Coverage is incomplete. Exhausting an available listing does not establish worldwide coverage, and rosters may contain unresolved or missing teams. Coverage information accompanies the selected population.

## Usage and builds

Usage divides complete registrations containing a Pokémon by all complete resolved registrations in the cohort. Each registration counts once, regardless of rounds or standing. Missing teams are excluded from the denominator.

Build distributions use registrations where that field is known. Move and teammate shares need not sum to 100%, because several can occur in one team. Teammate share uses complete registrations containing the selected Pokémon as its denominator. Missing values remain distinct from zero.

Canonical identities distinguish source species, held item, and derived form. A registered Mega stone establishes an available form, not proof that evolution occurred. Registration does not establish which Pokémon were brought or led, or which moves were used.

## Results and comparisons

Eligible outcomes are decisive competitive matches or series with both complete teams resolved. One best-of-three series is one physical result. Swiss/top cut and best-of-one/best-of-three events are pooled within compatible cohorts.

Ties, double losses, identifiable byes, automatic or administrative results, missing opponents, malformed records, unsupported phases, and ambiguous winners are excluded. Sources cannot identify every unmarked administrative result. A later drop does not remove earlier legitimate results.

Each physical result supplies two team perspectives. Win rate divides winning perspectives by all qualifying perspectives. A mirror can contribute one win and one loss while counting as one physical match. Overlapping rows cannot be summed to obtain a total.

Matchups accepts unordered combinations of one to six Pokémon. A team must contain every selected member. The conditional rate uses candidate-side perspectives whose opponent contains the target combination. The candidate's overall baseline uses that complete combination and identical filters, including the target observations; it never averages individual member rates.

Change versus overall is conditional win rate minus overall win rate, in **percentage points**. A change from 50% to 55% is +5 points. Unrounded rates determine ordering and group membership. Best includes finite changes of zero or greater; Worst includes negative changes. Discovery excludes the exact identical target combination.

## Samples and limitations

Automatic rankings use the configured floor of 20 physical matches, two events, and five distinct row-side participant identities. This is a provisional eligibility rule, not a confidence guarantee. Provider participant IDs do not establish unique humans across sources. Sparse manual comparisons remain inspectable.

Sample notices and their optional filter provide context without changing raw rates. Most evidence orders by physical matches, event count, and canonical identity; it does not certify reliability. Event concentration and shared matches create dependencies.

Results are observational and unadjusted for player skill, event selection, time, team composition, or repeated participants. A high-ranked combination discovered among many candidates is not independent predictive validation. Builds and teammate associations do not establish causes of wins.

## Publications

Collection saves source snapshots, normalizes and validates facts, then publishes a complete version atomically. Failed publication or validation preserves the prior publication and its original timestamps. Partial provider failures can publish refreshed coverage alongside carried-forward validated facts, with the coverage limits recorded. Browsing never collects providers.

Regulations and incompatible environments stay separate. Outgoing coverage enters Archive when a regulation ends, with one bounded final collection opportunity seven days later. Failed or missed finalization retains the last valid archive. [Ladder statistics](ladder-coverage.md) have separate periods and denominators.
